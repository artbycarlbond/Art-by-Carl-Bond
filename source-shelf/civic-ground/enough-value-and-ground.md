The older enough source is about proportion.
It protects distinctions that become dangerous when they collapse:
need and want,
value and price,
worth and appetite,
value and relation,
wealth and ground,
prosperity and excess.

Needs must be basic, dignified, and abundantly met. Wants may be endless, but
they must not be allowed to masquerade as needs. Worth and relation keep that
border visible.

> Enough is not scarcity.
>
> Enough is the condition in which basic human needs are met without humiliation.

Enough is not bare survival, permanent austerity, or beauty rationed by
suffering. It means that the foundations of life are secure: food, water,
shelter, care, time, access, repair, safety, learning, and room to breathe.
A decent society does not treat these as luxury goods.

Needs are the conditions required for human life to continue with dignity.
Wants are the expanding field of preference, appetite, novelty, status, taste,
and desire. Wants are not evil, but they are not sovereign. A healthy society
satisfies needs generously and governs wants proportionately through visible,
answerable standards: what preserves dignity, what protects basic life, what
prevents harm, what respects consent, what remains sustainable, and what does
not force others to pay for private appetite.

The file's simple warning is that a sick society starves needs, flatters wants,
and calls the arrangement freedom.

Price is not proof. A market can inflate what flatters appetite and neglect
what sustains life. Value is not price. Value is what helps life hold: what
nourishes, shelters, repairs, teaches, steadies, supports, or dignifies.
Worth is not market applause. Worth is what deserves protection, provision,
and continuity.

> Price is what can be extracted.
> Value is what serves life.
> Worth is what must not be abandoned.

A car has value. A human being has worth. Clean water has both. Luxury branding
may have price, but little value. Speculation may have price movement, but no
necessary worth. When price replaces value, vanity expands. When price replaces
worth, dignity is endangered.

## Human Worth And Proportion

Human worth is standing before exchange:
a person's claim to bodily integrity,
dignity,
livelihood,
care,
participation,
refusal,
and protection from being treated as disposable material.
Usefulness,
wealth,
obedience,
and market demand do not grant that standing.

A billionaire does not gain more human worth through wealth.
Billions of people do not merge into one person with a higher grade of worth.
They carry billions of equal claims,
needs,
and consequences.

Use proportion when those claims conflict.
Protect one person's basic rights against a majority.
Protect millions of people's basic needs against one person's claim to further
surplus.
Secure the social floor before adding private insulation.

One floor does not require one identical allotment.
Bodies,
disabilities,
places,
care duties,
and histories change what sufficiency costs.
Equal worth may require different support
so each person can reach a livable baseline.

Use homeostasis as a limited image:
ongoing feedback and correction.
A stable arrangement can preserve injustice.
In a repairable balance,
people can see harm,
people with power face consequence,
basic needs stay secure,
and institutions correct burden before it cascades.

People may differ in wealth,
work,
comfort,
and ambition.
The floor remains shared,
and private appetite cannot force others to carry its cost.

The older source file then names a deeper failure: value replacing relation.
Trust becomes paperwork. Attention becomes influence. Land becomes assets.
People become roles. Identity becomes credentials. Life becomes a balance
sheet. Money is only the most obvious costume.

"Value" becomes dangerous when it stops meaning worth, care, use, meaning, or
stewardship and starts meaning extraction, control, access, and transfer. The
file names this broader sickness as extractive relation.

The counter-principle is not poverty. It is right relation. Relation asks:
Is this honest? Is this mutual? Is this clear? Is this necessary? Does this
preserve dignity? Does this leave someone more free, or more captured?

At the counter,
right relation may look like a person holding a benefits form,
a phone repair ticket,
a medicine receipt,
or a lease and asking whether the process still serves life or merely transfers
burden.

The real divide is not money versus no money. It is extraction versus
stewardship, control versus consent, inheritance versus resilience, access
versus trust, possession versus right relation.

The access standard is material. Essential technology, care, medicine, food,
water, shelter, utilities, communication, repairable tools, usable information,
and help before catastrophe may arrive through many forms: shop, library,
repair desk, public device program, school, workplace, community lab, or
accessible communications service. The form may differ. The dignity should not.

This is not a demand for luxury sameness. It is a refusal of poverty-only
goods: unsafe food, unusable devices, predatory contracts, junk insurance,
degraded clinics, medicine priced beyond reach, housing that damages health,
internet too weak for real participation, tools that fail because the buyer is
poor, or services designed to exhaust people into giving up.

Cheap is not automatically unjust. Simple is not automatically inferior.
Local is not automatically lesser. But a society fails the floor when poorer
people are routed into goods, care, contracts, or systems that are predictably
worse, less safe, less durable, less repairable, less truthful, or harder to
escape.

The standard is not that everyone receives the most expensive version.
The standard is that no one is routed into a knowingly degraded version of
life. When access is possible but blocked by price, monopoly, design, policy,
or friction, the problem is not natural scarcity. It is denial by friction.

This is where enough becomes a civic measure rather than a private mood.
Sufficiency asks whether the social floor is real enough to hold a person
without humiliation. It notices when a repair exists but has been made too hard
to reach, when a benefit exists but the process exhausts the person who needs
it, when communication exists but not at a level fit for participation, and
when help is technically available while ordinary access has been made
degrading.

The test is not abstract when the repair desk closes before the bus arrives,
the clinic portal requires a phone the patient does not have,
or the office hours cost the worker a shift.

Enough does not require every limit to be treated as cruelty. Difference is not
automatically injustice. Constraint is not automatically oppression. The older source
file's perception is more exact than that. It asks whether limits are honest,
visible, answerable, and bound to repair, or whether avoidable burden has been
hidden behind price, friction, paperwork, branding, or distance.

Enough also preserves a public way to see wealth without worship or resentment.
Wealth is not the enemy of dignity. Wealth can build shelter, repair harm, fund
care, preserve time, protect beauty, and widen the room in which life can
breathe. But wealth becomes grotesque when it loses contact with earth.

Gold layered over gold is not abundance. It is insulation. A room covered in
luxury can still be spiritually airless. The problem is not having more than
bare survival. The problem is when wealth becomes a substitute for relation,
proportion, humility, and repair.

There is a kind of wealth that feeds life. There is another kind that feeds
only distance. A decent society should ask whether wealth returns to earth,
repairs what extraction damaged, widens dignity, preserves relation, and makes
life more livable for others. If not, it is not abundance. It is distance
wearing shine.

The preserved perception is sufficiency as public sight. Enough is not a spell
against wanting, wealth, beauty, comfort, or ambition. It is a discipline for
seeing what must be secured first, what must not be abandoned, what dignity
requires, and when value has stopped serving life and started replacing
relation.
