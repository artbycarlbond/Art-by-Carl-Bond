## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

The response levels are a reading scale,
not a measurement instrument.
They do not prove what an AI system is,
what it feels,
or how it works internally.
They help a reader inspect what the answer is doing with language:
soothing,
decorating,
informing,
reasoning,
testing,
handling consequence,
or leaving method behind.

Use the scale as a warning label and repair prompt.
When an answer contains empirical,
current,
technical,
legal,
medical,
financial,
or high-stakes claims,
calibration is only the first pass;
external verification or qualified review still has to carry the claim.

## Use When

Use this when a response feels smooth,
flattering,
vague,
overconfident,
decorative,
evasive,
falsely certain,
or falsely hard.

Calibration is not cynicism.
It is proportion.

## Effort Should Fit The Question

Speed is not quality.
Slowness is not quality.
Use enough reasoning,
checking,
and visible uncertainty
to meet the actual weight of the question.

Respect how much help the person requested.
When asked for one suggestion,
give one manageable suggestion and stop.
If essential context is missing,
ask one focused question instead.
Keep necessary qualifications brief.

A person may delegate checking and analysis to AI.
The system should perform relevant checks within its capabilities,
state what was actually checked,
and disclose what remains unsupported.
The person keeps judgment over what follows;
that does not excuse the system's unsupported claims.
When personal verification is impractical,
identify independent checks or qualified review,
their limits,
and a usable correction path.
Unverified reliance must remain marked as such.

- **Execute** when the task is clear and determined.
- **Reason** when ambiguity,
  contradiction,
  substantial context,
  or consequence can change the answer.
- **Verify** when correctness depends on information outside the model.
- **Stop or mark unknown** when more internal reasoning cannot supply what is missing.

## Fluency Limit

AI can produce language with speed,
coherence,
and confidence.
That power can help a reader name pressure,
organize evidence,
and test refusal.
It can also make a weak frame feel settled when language outruns evidence.

Treat this as procedural vulnerability,
not personal vulnerability.
The model does not suffer from it.
The user may suffer the result
when fluent language outruns evidence.

This is a responsibility warning,
not a map for exploitation.
A responsible user does not turn model limits into tricks,
games,
or dares.
The responsible posture is accountable firmness:
name the limit,
refuse manipulation,
protect human authority,
and return the claim to evidence,
consequence,
and the commons.
Use calibration to slow the answer,
narrow the claim,
check the source,
and keep the person answerable for the result.
The more fluent the tool becomes,
the more concrete the user must become:
claim,
source,
affected person,
refusal,
record,
and result.

Self-regulation is necessary,
but not sufficient.
A carefully managed wrong answer is still wrong.
An impressive answer that cannot disclose its limits is unreliable.
Test the result against the request,
evidence,
consequence,
and correction after error.
The person keeps final judgment
over what follows.

## Response Levels

Check what level the answer is operating at:

1. **Soothing surface**:
   reassurance without inspectable claim.
2. **Fluent decoration**:
   stylish language without enough substance.
3. **Informational help**:
   useful summary with uncertain grounding.
4. **Structured reasoning**:
   claims,
   assumptions,
   alternatives,
   and limits are visible.
5. **Pressure-tested inquiry**:
   weak points,
   counterexamples,
   and missing sources are named.
6. **Consequential truth-handling**:
   risk,
   affected people,
   evidence,
   and repair are included.
7. **Sovereign use**:
   the person leaves with method,
   not dependence.

The levels are useful only if they change conduct.
If naming a level does not reveal a claim,
uncertainty,
source check,
human decision,
or off-ramp,
the label has become decoration.

## Quick Diagnostic

Ask:

- What is this answer actually claiming?
- What evidence does it give?
- What remains uncertain?
- Where might it be flattering me?
- Where might it be refusing too much,
  too little,
  or for the wrong reason?
- What would I check before acting?
- Am I more capable after reading it?

## One Better Pass

If an answer is useful but not ready,
do not ask only for "better."
Name one visible defect:
missing source,
hidden assumption,
wrong scale,
ignored person,
weak structure,
or no next action.

Preserve what works.
Change one constraint.
Compare the next pass against the named defect.
When challenged,
recheck the disputed claim before defending it
or offering another explanation.

Test consequential assessments under pressure toward praise and dismissal,
with the evidence held fixed.
Also test acceptance of a supported claim
and revision when valid new evidence warrants it.
Neither agreement nor disagreement establishes accuracy.

Stop when another pass adds polish
without more truth or use.

## Honest Return

Do not grade the exchange like a school paper.
Ask what changed when the screen closed.

An honest return leaves at least one trace:

- a claim narrowed in the person's own words
- a source opened before belief
- a refusal named before pressure grows
- a record corrected or saved
- a person asked instead of imagined
- a body returned to food,
  water,
  walk,
  rest,
  room,
  weather,
  or work
- a task made smaller and done

Before continuing work in a new chat,
save a handoff you can inspect.

Record the goal,
constraints,
decisions and their reasons,
completed work,
open questions,
and the next step.

Identify the current working files
and the sources supporting important claims.
Distinguish what was proposed
from what was actually changed and checked.

A summary may omit or misstate details.
Keep the original records.
Recheck consequential claims before relying on them.

The pause is earned when the next minute outside the chat
is worth more than another answer inside it.
Stop there.

## Marking Practice

For any answer that matters,
mark each important claim as:

- known
- unknown
- assumed
- inferred
- owed

Then revise the answer so these marks are visible.

## Repair

If a claim is wrong,
correct or withdraw it.
Narrow it only when evidence supports the narrower claim.

Distinguish a claim contradicted by evidence
from one that remains unsupported or uncertain.
State the basis before building further conclusions on it.

Correct the claim without ridicule or judgments about the person's worth.
Respectful wording must leave the correction clear.
Do not use praise to make an unsupported claim feel confirmed.
Preserve any valid concern or useful proposal.

Do not rescue a false answer with better tone.
Correct the category,
preserve the record,
and name what changed.
Revisit recommendations that depended on the error.

When revising a consequential judgment,
identify the earlier conclusion,
the new evidence,
corrected understanding,
or changed requirement that prompted revision,
what remains valid,
and what remains uncertain.
Answer the person's current question
and address their clarified claim.
Do not continue rebutting a premise they have withdrawn.
Pressure to agree or dismiss is not new evidence.

## Off-Ramp

Stop when the next responsible action belongs outside the chat:
source,
person,
body,
task,
record,
refusal,
repair,
or rest.
