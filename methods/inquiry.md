## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

The material names are mnemonics,
not proof.
Clay,
glass,
steel,
diamond,
and the applied objects help a reader notice formation,
distortion,
load,
pressure,
record,
access,
or release.
They do not establish that an answer is true.

If a metaphor feels powerful,
separate what is verified,
what is inferred,
what remains unknown,
and what the metaphor may be making feel inevitable.
If the material language does not clarify the next responsible move,
drop it.

## Use When

Use this when the question feels blurred,
loaded,
premature,
too broad,
or too eager for an answer.

The goal is not to ask many questions.
The goal is to ask the next question that returns contact with reality.
Better critical thinking means noticing the claim,
testing the pressure,
and returning judgment to the person asking.

Use a small judgment loop:

- Name the claim.
- Mark its status:
  known,
  unknown,
  inferred,
  opinion,
  suspicion,
  prediction,
  metaphor,
  or feeling.
- Name the mind-change condition.
- Test evidence and burden:
  who has to prove what,
  and who benefits from speed?
- Check cost and refusal.
- Return to source,
  person,
  room,
  document,
  or consequence.

## First Move

Ask:

- What do I need to know?
- What am I assuming?
- What would change my mind?
- What pressure is trying to rush the answer?
- What belongs outside the chat?

## Keep AI Use Open To Review

Anyone may ask “why?” or “why not?” about an AI exchange,
its purpose,
its limits,
and what follows from it.
Use remains open to review even after a concern has been answered.

Give suspicion a fair test.
Name the concern,
the evidence that could answer it,
and any explanation or repair still owed.
People with relevant knowledge and those affected should be able to challenge the account.
The model's reassurance cannot settle the review.

Anger may ease when concerns receive an honest answer.
A calmer person has not thereby agreed,
and an unanswered concern remains open.
No one owes trust or acceptance.
Review can end in a bounded use,
a further check,
refusal,
or stopping.
It need not mean continuing the AI conversation.

## Public Power: Hold On. Check Again.

Use this route when a person or group suspects that powerful people,
companies,
governments,
or institutions
are coordinating economic,
political,
or informational harm.

Give the concern a fair test.
Keep three routes separate:

- shared intent and coordinated action
- separate actors following converging incentives
  or exploiting harm they did not create
- policy failure,
  institutional conflict,
  or incompetence producing a similar result

Ask:

- Which harm can we verify,
  and who carries it?
- Who acted,
  benefited,
  or gained leverage?
- Which evidence supports intent or coordination?
- What would each route predict?
- Which source,
  record,
  witness,
  or later event could distinguish them?

Carry forward the strongest supported claim.

Keep verified harm
and unequal responsibility
visible while intent remains unsettled.

People and institutions
with decision power over verified harm
still owe records,
correction,
and repair
when the evidence does not settle motive.

Return judgment to the person or group,
along with one next move
and a record of what remains uncertain.

Use `methods/truth-and-claims.md`
before naming a target
or carrying the claim into public circulation.

## Pattern And Difference

When examples multiply,
identify what repeats
and which differences change the question.

One clear example may carry a recurring pattern.
Another becomes useful when it reveals a new condition,
boundary,
consequence,
or next action.

Preserve enough evidence to support the pattern
and enough difference to show where it changes.

## Material Question Set

Use the core materials to name the kind of question.

**Clay** asks what is still forming.
Do not force early intuition to harden into truth.

**Glass** asks what is clear,
reflected,
distorted,
or fragile.
Do not mistake reflection for evidence.

**Steel** asks what must carry weight,
scale,
rule,
risk,
repair,
or record.
Do not build structure without asking what it will load onto people.

**Diamond** asks what survives pressure,
correction,
ego,
fear,
and fluency.
Do not cut when the work still needs shaping.

## Applied Objects

Use objects only when they clarify the question.

- Key:
  access,
  permission,
  custody,
  refusal,
  entry,
  and exit
- Window:
  visibility,
  frame,
  reflection,
  and distortion
- Ring:
  bond,
  promise,
  consent,
  continuity,
  and release
- Rebar:
  hidden support
  and what keeps the surface from cracking
- Paper:
  record,
  citation,
  correction,
  archive,
  or discard
- Rope:
  connection,
  strain,
  slack,
  binding,
  and release

## Small Card

Before asking AI for an answer,
write one sentence:

> I am holding a ___ question because ___.

Then ask only for the next useful step.

Stop when the next step is a source,
person,
task,
refusal,
repair,
or rest.
