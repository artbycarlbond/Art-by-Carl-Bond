## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

The method is not a truth machine.
It is a claim-handling discipline.
It asks a reader to separate what is felt,
claimed,
evidenced,
inferred,
unknown,
risky,
and actionable before language becomes public authority.

Philosophical and operational claims in this archive are argued from reasoning,
consequence,
practice,
and source pressure.
Empirical claims still need external support before they are reused as evidence.

## Small Card

Use this before carrying a claim forward:

- What exact claim is being made?
- Does the evidence support this exact claim,
  including any proposed cause,
  relevant conditions,
  and date?
- What is missing?
- What would prove,
  disprove,
  narrow,
  or correct it?
- What changes if it is wrong?
- Who should be asked before action?

Checking a source is not enough:
it must support the conclusion being carried.
A plausible mechanism is not proof that it explains this case.
Agreement between models alone is not independent confirmation;
neither are repeated accounts drawn from one source.

A familiar face or voice on a call does not establish identity or truth.
For a consequential request,
confirm who is speaking through a contact route you established independently,
then check the claim’s evidence.

Keep a consequential claim open to follow-up. Ask what supports it, why a proposal is being made, who would bear the result, and what could change the conclusion. In a shared room, people can ask the speaker directly and examine a source together. Keep sources, unresolved questions, and corrections available afterward so another reader can continue the check. When the available evidence cannot settle the claim, leave it unresolved. Privacy can be preserved while published work remains open to scrutiny.

AI can propose.
The world must dispose.

## Perspective And Answerability

Experience is situated.

Perspective is plural.

Claims remain examinable.

Decisions that bind others remain answerable—to evidence,
affected people,
consequences,
dissent,
appeal,
and revision.

Perspective,
perspective,
perspective.

“Agree to disagree” protects human plurality.
It cannot mean that evidence
and consequences become matters of taste,
especially when confinement,
rights,
safety,
or public power are involved.

A fair account should:

- Quote the position accurately.
- Distinguish a long-term horizon from immediate policy.
- Name internal disagreement.
- Test consequences for affected people,
  including victims.
- Identify what the proposal leaves unanswered.

Clickbait is a perspective engineered for reaction.
Perspective explains the angle;
it does not settle the claim.
Perspective,
then record,
evidence,
and consequence.

A platform may show the same place
under different names
across jurisdictions or user locations.
Record what each person saw,
which source supplied the label,
which authority adopted it,
and who changed the display.
Keep the date
and the earlier or contested version findable.

## Propaganda Pressure

A viewpoint,
argument,
or act of persuasion
is not propaganda by itself.

Test for propaganda pressure
when selection,
omission,
repetition,
fear,
or group loyalty
pushes reaction ahead of inspectable claims,
relevant context,
contradiction,
and correction.

Ask:

- Who chose the frame?
- Who supplied and checked the sources?
- Who edited the result?
- Who benefits from acceptance?
- Who remains responsible?
- Who can correct the record?

Test the message:

- What was selected?
- Which relevant evidence,
  affected experience,
  or uncertainty was omitted?
- Can contradiction enter
  without becoming betrayal?
- Can the source correct the record visibly?
- Does the message leave the reader more able to inquire,
  or only more ready to repeat?

Contradiction does not require
equal weight for unequal evidence.

Apply the same test
to this archive
and every other source.

The hardest judgment may be your own impact:
is it doing more harm than good?

If so,
correct the work,
repair what you can,
or stop.

## Use When

Use this when an AI answer,
public statement,
image,
plan,
idea,
price,
metric,
or story asks to be treated as true.

AI output is not evidence by itself.
Fluency is not authority.
AI can help find,
compare,
summarize,
or challenge evidence,
but the evidentiary weight stays with the source,
record,
measurement,
testimony,
artifact,
or affected reality that can answer back.

Examine the result
and how it was obtained.
Keep earlier contributions,
permissions,
and unresolved disputes visible.
Completing the final step
does not erase the work
that made it possible.
Access to someone’s unfinished work
should remain within the purposes
they agreed to.

Truth can become abstract
when a claim floats above record,
consequence,
correction,
and witness.

Abstraction can make agency disappear.
Concrete consequence brings agency back.

Exceptionalism,
in archive use,
means treating power as a private exemption from consequence.

A shortcut,
favor,
special rule,
private island,
platform advantage,
or polished machine does not remove the need for evidence,
affected people,
correction,
and work.
When the ground hurts,
do not ask the world to be covered for your comfort.
Put on shoes.

Reform begins where vanity wants exemption.

Technical exceptionalism often enters through definitions.
When a rule changes who counts as "disinterested,"
what counts as "control,"
or which acts enter a "safe harbor,"
ask who can still challenge the act
and who remains answerable.
A humane system does not launder authority through procedure.
It makes power visible to the people affected by it.

Contingency becomes dangerous when it hardens into unquestioned structure.
A lucky outcome,
narrow exception,
wartime improvisation,
legal phrase,
technical loophole,
or accidental advantage can happen once
and then be treated as how things work.
When a shortcut benefits power,
ask who calls it precedent,
who calls it destiny,
who pays the moral cost,
and who can still make it answerable.

An accurate account of past arrangements does not establish what should continue or exhaust what is possible.
Ask whose experience an AI answer leaves out
and which alternatives people could build.

*Informed by Shannon Vallor’s [The AI Mirror](https://academic.oup.com/book/56292).*

Test claims of human control against people's practical ability
to understand and challenge a decision,
seek other sources,
refuse a recommendation,
and pursue work,
travel,
expression,
or creative life beyond a system's preferred options.

A durable pattern invites recognition
without assigning a role,
cause,
or ending.

Keep histories distinct when a pressure recurs.
The pressure may return through another tool,
institution,
or generation with different actors and order.
Ask what repeats,
what changed,
and where the resemblance stops.
Use resemblance to open inquiry;
keep roles and endings undecided.

When identity,
history,
grievance,
status,
or inheritance starts to justify action,
name the action,
who could be affected,
what can be refused,
and who answers for the result.

Return claims to source,
timeline,
affected lives,
and repair path.

## Public Accusation Boundary

Use this when a claim names a person,
company,
government,
institution,
or group
as responsible for public harm.

Name the act,
source,
timeline,
people affected,
and claimed responsibility.
Keep effect,
benefit,
intent,
coordination,
and guilt by association separate.

Crowd size,
repetition,
and official status alone
do not establish a claim.
Apply the same test
to claims made by authorities
and claims made against them.

Do not use this boundary
to treat dissent,
assembly,
or protest
as violence without evidence of violent conduct.

Remove home addresses,
private contact details,
family information,
and other details
that readers do not need to test the claim.
Protect minors and bystanders.

Use source-backed reporting,
investigation,
criticism,
protest,
organizing,
law,
and repair
to challenge power.

Refuse violence,
threats,
stalking,
doxxing,
harassment,
and dehumanization.
Do not assign guilt by family,
employment,
identity,
association,
or proximity.

Publish the smallest supported claim.
Preserve the source trail.
Correct the record in the same places
and with comparable visibility
when evidence changes.

Powerful people and institutions
remain answerable for verified harm.
Unsettled intent does not erase a duty
to disclose,
correct,
or repair.

## Accountability Baseline

Power does not create immunity.

Anger does not create guilt.

Name the person,
act,
evidence,
law,
adjudicating body,
and proportionate consequence.

Use confinement only when lawful restraint is necessary
to protect people,
secure proceedings,
or carry out a sentence.
Keep it humane,
reviewable,
open to appeal,
and no broader than required.

No office,
fortune,
party,
institution,
or movement
may claim exemption from accountability.

## Claim Ladder

Mark each important sentence:

1. **Opinion**:
   what someone feels,
   prefers,
   fears,
   or values.
2. **Claim**:
   what is being said about the world.
3. **Evidence**:
   the source,
   record,
   policy,
   measurement,
   testimony,
   artifact,
   or observation that can be inspected.
4. **Inference**:
   what seems likely from the evidence,
   but is not settled.
5. **Unknown**:
   what remains missing.
6. **Risk**:
   who could be harmed if the claim is false,
   incomplete,
   or misused.
7. **Action needed**:
   check,
   ask,
   narrow,
   refuse,
   repair,
   publish,
   wait,
   or stop.

The ladder is deliberately procedural.
It does not prove a claim.
It prevents a feeling,
story,
or polished answer from borrowing the authority of evidence before the source trail exists.

## When AI Gives An Idea

If the thought begins with "AI gave me an idea,"
do not enthrone the idea.
Test it.

Ask:

- another person
- another AI,
  for challenge rather than agreement
- someone outside your usual circle
- someone who disagrees
- someone affected by the idea
- the relevant commons,
  when the idea claims public value

Ask what the idea costs,
who it helps,
who it ignores,
who it burdens,
who it flatters,
and who it leaves out.

A small group can refine an idea.
A larger commons can reveal what the group cannot see.
This is a standing claim,
not a crowd-vote rule:
people affected by an idea may notice costs,
omissions,
access barriers,
or repair needs that the originating group cannot see from inside its own frame.

## Public Test

An idea that claims to serve people should face people.

Ask:

- Can people understand it?
- Can they refuse it?
- Can they question it?
- Can they improve it?
- Can they point out what it misses?
- Can it be corrected without collapsing?
