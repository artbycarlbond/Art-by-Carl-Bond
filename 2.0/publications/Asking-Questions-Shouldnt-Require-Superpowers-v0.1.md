# Asking Questions Shouldn't Require Superpowers

## Football, changing weather, and keeping the ability to continue

Version 0.1 · November 2, 2026  
A field note and practical companion to [AI Continuity Kit v0.1](AI-Continuity-Kit-v0.1.md). Developed through dialogue with ChatGPT. The analogies and exercises are proposals for discussion; they have not been validated as a general recovery method.

Asking questions should be an ordinary, accessible way to explore an idea. Starting a project, clarifying an answer, or correcting a misunderstanding should not demand superhuman patience.

People can feel stopped by an AI refusal or by an assistant that ignores corrections. The useful question is what would make the next step understandable and manageable. That responsibility belongs partly to the product and its designers. Users should not have to keep repairing a service indefinitely.

## 1. Football: a sack interrupts the play

In American football, a sack stops the quarterback behind the line of scrimmage. It can mean lost ground, frustration, and a disrupted plan. A failed AI interaction can feel similar: someone approaches with a goal and leaves with less momentum and no clear next move.

The analogy helps when it prompts a review of the interrupted play. What was the goal? What blocked progress? Is there a reasonable next attempt?

| Football image | Useful meaning for an AI interaction |
| --- | --- |
| The intended play | The user's actual goal |
| The sack | A refusal, ignored correction, or failed attempt that interrupts progress |
| Reviewing the play | Examining the request, response, available information, and tools |
| Choosing the next play | Clarifying, supplying a missing source, reducing the task, or choosing another tool |
| Ending the attempt | Deciding that further troubleshooting is not worth the effort |

The analogy has limits. AI assistance is not inherently a contest, and the objective is not to defeat the system. A refusal can be justified, mistaken, or poorly explained. An assistant can also agree to do something and then perform it incorrectly. Getting a “yes” does not establish success.

A useful recovery question is:

> My goal is [goal]. What specific obstacle prevents you from helping, and what useful part can you complete with the information and tools available?

The answer should help distinguish a misunderstanding, missing information, unavailable access, a capability limit, or a restriction. Its explanation still needs examination; the assistant may misdescribe its own limitations.

Someone who stops after a failure has not failed a character test. Their time, energy, accessibility needs, and previous experience matter. A usable product should make recovery easier.

## 2. Rain: the model changes, while the question can remain useful

Imagine changing models and product behavior as changing weather. A method that worked yesterday may need adjustment under different conditions. Instructions may be interpreted differently, a tool may be unavailable, or the result may change in quality or tone. OpenAI's own prompting guidance notes that different model versions can produce different results from the same approach. [1]

Some questions remain useful through those changes:

- What evidence supports this claim?
- Which of my requirements did this result miss?
- What information is still missing?
- What would demonstrate that the task is complete?

These questions travel well because they identify things a reader or assistant can examine. The exact phrasing may still need adjustment. There is no weatherproof sentence that guarantees a correct response from every model.

In this analogy, preserving source files, decisions, and examples is like protecting the work from the weather. The record lets someone resume or inspect it later. It does not freeze the service or preserve an identical assistant.

This is also where “rust” can mislead. Asking many questions does not itself create permanent memory or immunity to model changes. ChatGPT's memory is selective and does not retain every detail. [2] A copy you keep yourself provides a separate record; the next assistant must actually receive the relevant material to use it.

## 3. Inquiry can open a possibility; insistence cannot guarantee truth

A question can turn a vague difficulty into a specific investigation. Asking “Which requirement failed?” creates a more useful task than repeatedly demanding “Do better.” That can open room for an unexpected solution or a moment of understanding.

But the number or force of requests cannot guarantee correctness. Repeating a demand until an assistant agrees can leave the underlying claim unexamined. Ask for something that can be checked.

| Kind of task | A useful check |
| --- | --- |
| Factual question | Does the cited source actually support the claim, with relevant dates and qualifications? |
| Calculation | Can the result be reproduced from the stated inputs? |
| Document revision | Were the required facts and approved decisions preserved? |
| Creative work | Does the result satisfy the agreed intent and constraints? |
| Action using a tool | Is there evidence that the action succeeded? |
| Unresolved question | Does the response clearly identify what remains unknown? |

An honest unresolved answer can be the correct outcome. A creative task may have several acceptable outcomes. The standard should fit the task.

## 4. A small example of useful pushback

Suppose a person asks for a revision to a period illustration. The approved choices are a 1939 setting, a woman scientist taking an active role, no lettering, and clear space for a later masthead. A new result introduces modern equipment and generated lettering.

The next request can make the disagreement concrete:

> Keep the approved 1939 setting and the woman scientist's active role. Remove the lettering and preserve the clear masthead space. Revise those elements without changing the composition. If any requirement cannot be met, identify it specifically.

Then examine the actual revision. If the same defects remain, record the failure and consider a different method or tool. The model's statement that it followed the request is not the check.

This example illustrates a method; it is not evidence that every image generator will follow it successfully.

## 5. Ordinary inquiry includes judgment

AI can generate questions as well as answers. The value of inquiry depends on choosing a relevant question, interpreting the reply, checking it, and deciding what follows.

A person asking repeated questions may be doing several kinds of work: defining the goal, identifying contradictions, rejecting an unsuitable interpretation, and deciding whether an answer matters in practice. These are learnable activities. They need not be treated as an extraordinary personal gift or an obligation to become a technical specialist.

Research also documents difficulties. A 2023 study of ten people designing chatbots found that participants often generalized from individual successes or failures and brought expectations from human conversation into prompt design. That limited study does not explain everyone's experience, but it gives concrete reasons to improve instruction and interface design. [3]

## 6. Agency includes the choice to stop

Here, “sovereign agency” means retaining practical choices: question an answer, correct a misunderstanding, examine evidence, preserve work, choose another approach, or leave.

It does not require accepting every answer, obtaining agreement at any cost, or concealing the purpose of a request. It also does not require tolerating endless friction. A user can reasonably conclude that a service is unsuitable for a task.

Providers should offer understandable explanations, dependable handling of corrections, portable records, and clear notices when changes affect ongoing work. These are proposed expectations for usable services. A personal prompt cannot substitute for them.

## 7. Make the experience useful to someone else

An archive entry can remain simple:

1. State the question and intended result.
2. Show a short, anonymized example of the problem.
3. Record one clarification or attempted correction.
4. Show the actual result, including continued failure.
5. Explain what the example establishes and what remains uncertain.

Record the date and the visible model or tool label when available. Use “unknown” when necessary. Keep the original material needed to assess the example. Repeating the task can help distinguish a recurring problem from one unusual response; passing a few checks is still limited evidence. Evaluation benefits from realistic tasks and human judgment. [4]

The companion Continuity Kit supplies a restart card, checkpoint, and starter checks. This note explains why someone might use them. Each person should keep private context separate from a public template.

For a community discussion, acknowledge the specific difficulty first. Offer a manageable experiment without implying that people caused the problem by asking badly. A successful personal experience does not disprove another person's failure, and an unsuccessful example does not establish that every capability has disappeared.
