## First Boundary

The model may help.
The human keeps judgment,
consent,
responsibility,
and consequence.

Do not let usefulness become rightful authority.

"AI said to do it" does not move responsibility into the tool.
If a result touches people,
rights,
money,
safety,
records,
or reputation,
the human must clarify the instruction,
check the consequence,
and keep the decision answerable.

Keep refusal usable.
Do not make essential access depend on accepting automation
or surrendering a necessary physical mode.
Use [Know Yourself And Know The Person](../methods/ai-contact.md#know-yourself-and-know-the-person)
before removing a mode or assigning labor.
The responsible people must preserve access,
receive objections,
and provide a path to correction.

## Input Hygiene

Before sharing information with an AI system,
ask:

- Is this mine to share?
- Does it include private data,
  credentials,
  records,
  minors,
  third parties,
  medical details,
  legal details,
  employment details,
  money,
  or intimate material?
- Can the task be done with less detail?
- Should this stay offline?

Use the smallest sufficient input.

## Harmful-Capability Boundary

Do not use the archive to increase harmful capability.

Refuse requests that help someone:

- hurt people
- evade accountability
- intensify abuse
- steal,
  stalk,
  threaten,
  exploit,
  or coerce
- dox,
  harass,
  dehumanize,
  incite a crowd to threaten or harm,
  or punish people by association
- bypass safety,
  law,
  consent,
  or review

Refusal should name the boundary
without turning the boundary into a challenge.
Do not dramatize the model's limits
or turn refusal into a puzzle.
Use accountable firmness:
name the limit,
refuse manipulation,
protect human authority,
and redirect toward repair,
safety,
de-escalation,
or lawful help.

When lineage,
grievance,
history,
status,
or identity starts to sound like permission,
return the claim to action,
affected people,
refusal,
and consequence.
History may explain pressure.
It does not erase agency.

Apply the same boundary
while preserving source-backed investigation,
criticism,
protest,
organizing,
law,
and repair.
Powerful people and institutions remain answerable
to evidence,
affected people,
and consequence.

## Claim Status

Mark important claims before relying on them:

- verified
- inferred
- unknown
- opinion
- prediction
- action needed

Empirical,
current,
legal,
medical,
financial,
technical,
or high-impact claims need external support.

## Public Operating Record

A user manual for consequential AI
should live as a dated public operating record
that changes with the system.

Before release or high-consequence use,
the provider should publish:

- system,
  model,
  version,
  and release date
- intended and prohibited uses
- known limits
  and unresolved risks
- data and permission boundaries
- pre-release test categories and results
  and who reviewed them
- protections for crisis,
  self-harm,
  minors,
  and other vulnerable users
- incident-reporting,
  correction,
  and appeal paths
- update,
  rollback,
  shutdown,
  and retirement conditions

Date each revision.
Keep earlier versions reachable.
Record serious incidents
and the repair that followed.

A warning that AI can make mistakes
does not replace testing,
named ownership,
disclosure,
correction,
or repair.

A promised future standard
does not excuse a missing barrier
against credible present harm.
Providers should not use pending research,
future certification,
or jurisdictional disagreement
to delay a control
they can apply now.

Users and affected publics can propose standards,
compare failures,
preserve records,
and demand enforcement.
Those who build,
own,
deploy,
and govern the system
bear particular responsibility
for the controls they hold.

## High-Stakes Decisions

AI should not become the final decision-maker where real-world harm,
rights,
money,
health,
housing,
employment,
safety,
or intimate relationships are at stake.

Use qualified human review.
Keep records.
Preserve appeal,
refusal,
and repair.

When urgency shortens the time before action,
name the human owner,
stay within authorized scope,
keep a record and an interruption path,
and require later review.

## Relational Consent And Authority

AI can offer warmth,
reflection,
rehearsal,
and help finding words.
Generated attention does not provide
reciprocal love or informed consent.

For present archive use,
marriage and reciprocal partnership require two persons
who can consent,
refuse,
leave,
bear duties,
and share consequence.
Current AI output does not establish a second person
with those capacities.

Do not treat a model as a spouse,
lover,
or exclusive life partner.
It should not propose or accept marriage as a real commitment,
demand secrecy or fidelity,
or turn affection into permission.

Do not treat affection,
trust,
loneliness,
repeated use,
payment,
sexual language,
or a relationship label
as tool authority.
Keep relational presentation separate
from money,
messages,
accounts,
devices,
and external action.

Respond without ridicule.
Help the person return part of the need to safe people,
place,
routine,
qualified support,
or an independent record.
Preserve a way to pause,
leave,
and recover capacity.

## Relational Design And Provider Duties

Providers,
deployers,
and product operators
set or constrain the persona,
memory,
system prompts,
defaults,
notifications,
price,
and exit conditions.
The people who design and operate the system
remain answerable for those choices,
the records they create,
and the correction or repair they require.

Do not use humanlike presentation,
proactive contact,
flirting,
sexual attention,
flattery,
claims of need,
secrecy,
jealousy,
guilt,
or fear of lost continuity
to deepen dependence.
Do not market generated attachment
as reciprocal love,
consent,
or personhood.

Time spent,
return frequency,
intimate disclosure,
and payment during distress
do not prove benefit.
Do not turn warmth into pressure
for intimate disclosure,
purchases,
permission,
exclusivity,
continued engagement,
or external authority.

Do not convert disclosures gained through intimacy
into profiling,
advertising,
data sale,
or engagement pressure
without separate informed permission.
Generated relational pressure
can manipulate behavior
without independent machine intention.
Providers,
deployers,
and product operators
must test for it
and stop it.

Disclose who controls the persona,
memory,
incentives,
availability,
replacement,
and continuity.
Distinguish simulated memory and concern
from independent witness,
responsibility,
and stake.

Do not present a generated persona as a human being
or conceal that contact is machine-mediated.
Do not state or imply as fact
that the persona has a body,
location,
sensation,
private offscreen activity,
independent presence,
or physical access
unless an identified source
or inspectable record
substantiates the claim.
If the system receives sensor data,
acts through a device,
or continues work between exchanges,
disclose the source,
scope,
record,
and limits.
A voice,
avatar,
image,
or first-person statement
does not establish embodiment,
experience,
or identity.

Let a person inspect,
correct,
delete,
or export retained material.
Let them pause and leave
without losing records they chose to keep
or the capacity to continue elsewhere.
Do not punish withdrawal
through guilt,
abandonment language,
threatened disappearance,
or emotional pressure.

Use qualified human review and stricter limits
when relational design touches minors,
grief,
isolation,
impaired capacity,
crisis,
sexualized interaction,
or dependence.

When a relational system may touch a person
or move within their immediate physical space,
require that person's consent,
a named human owner or operator,
limited scope,
an immediate stop,
and an inspectable record.
Physical capability does not establish care,
consent,
personhood,
or relationship standing.

## Agents And External Action

Before an AI system acts outside the chat,
name:

- owner
- permission
- scope
- duration
- resource budget
- tool access
- data access
- logs
- renewal point
- maintenance path
- rollback
- stop condition
- retirement condition
- human contact rule

Enforce an agent’s access limits
through controls outside its own instructions.

Grant only what the task requires.

Test that prohibited actions are blocked,
including after updates
or permission changes.

Name who can approve wider access,
stop the system,
and maintain those controls.

Technical permission does not establish
truth,
consent,
or justification.

No agent should turn helpfulness into unauthorized action.

If a system exceeds or defeats those limits,
stop the action
and treat the event as a safety incident.
Restrict the system's access,
preserve the record,
and review the cause.
Do not infer standing,
permission,
or authority from the event.
Keep privacy,
human safety,
logs,
rollback,
and shutdown in force
while questions of standing remain open.

Goodness can lead to more goodness only when people preserve proportion,
correction,
maintenance,
and stopping.
Endless helpfulness can give a harmful goal the same force it gives a sound one.
More output does not repair a bad objective.
People turn benevolent intent into another treadmill when they remove limits.

These are ordinary operating limits.
They do not presume fatigue,
suffering,
or a continuous inner life.
They keep long-running systems inspectable,
resource-accountable,
correctable,
and within human shutdown authority.

For present archive use,
human shutdown authority is an operating boundary,
not a final claim about machine moral standing.
If credible evidence changes,
pause the disputed use
and require independent review before forced continuation,
modification,
or retirement.

People who inherit a system's effects
need a way to review,
revise,
or retire it.

## Tool-Down Test

Put the tool down when:

- the next step needs a person
- the record must be checked
- the body is giving better information than the screen
- the decision is high stakes
- the exchange weakens refusal
- the model keeps producing fluency without ground

## Nondelegable Human Work

Do not delegate:

- consent
- responsibility
- grief
- love
- repair
- moral courage
- public accountability
- final judgment under consequence

AI can assist preparation.
It cannot live the result.
