## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

A gathering anchor is a shared subject,
meal,
object,
task,
or quiet practice that gives people somewhere to place attention.

People can use the anchor to lower the demand to perform a personality,
fill every silence,
or disclose private pain before trust exists.
People can begin by noticing,
serving,
holding,
making,
repairing,
listening,
or sitting nearby.

Use it as a room-and-practice test.
It cannot cure loneliness,
define every third space,
or prove that an in-person gathering is humane.
The test is whether people can enter without a performance,
take part without capture,
leave without punishment,
and return with more standing and capacity.

## Use When

Use this practice when a gathering feels vague,
forced,
screen-led,
or dependent on icebreakers and disclosure.

Use it when planning a conversation table,
shared meal,
repair hour,
quiet room,
creative session,
class,
neighborhood meeting,
or AI-assisted image review.

Start with one gathering,
one primary anchor,
one low-pressure way to join,
and one reason to return.
Use the [four anchors](#four-working-anchors) to name what carries attention.
Use the [twelve civic doorways](#twelve-civic-doorways) to find an existing place to begin.
For a ten-minute plan, use the [drill](#drill-one-host-one-doorway-one-return)
and the [stop conditions](#stop-conditions).

## The Third Thing

People often gather with less pressure when they can turn toward something together.

People can use a question to set a shared direction.
They can use a meal to set a bodily pace.
A damaged object offers material limits that help people choose roles.
A song,
image,
book,
or quiet task gives people somewhere to rest attention.

People can place some social pressure on the third thing instead of carrying it alone.
Someone can enter through a small observation:

> I can hold that.
>
> I noticed this part.
>
> Pass the bowl.
>
> Let us look once more.

The person does not have to explain a whole life before taking part or becoming known.

A stranger does not have to become a friend
to stop being scenery.

Repeated,
low-pressure contact
can let people recognize one another
without demanding intimacy,
agreement,
disclosure,
or permanence.

A person should be able to return
and be recognized
without being tracked,
obligated,
or punished for absence.

## Four Working Anchors

For **Attention**,
place a question,
image,
text,
sound,
map,
or shared screen at the center.
People notice,
compare,
question,
and revise.
Keep the subject available to everyone in the room.

For **Hospitality**,
place food,
drink,
serving,
and clearing at the center.
Serving and eating set a bodily pace with natural pauses.
Food may support another activity,
or it may become the main activity for a while.

For **Stewardship**,
place something that needs care at the center.
A bicycle,
chair,
plant,
tool shelf,
notice board,
or shared room offers bounded work with visible consequence.
Someone inspects,
someone assists,
someone watches the whole,
and the roles can change.

For **Creation**,
place unfinished work at the center.
A print,
meal,
booklet,
song,
plan,
or public note gives people a material path for imagination.
The work can continue across meetings and give later visitors something to encounter.

These are working handles.
A real gathering may combine them.
Choose one as the center so people can tell what the room is asking of them.

## Twelve Civic Doorways

Use the twelve doorways to name familiar reasons to enter civic life.
They do not require twelve buildings.
Keep some open to people who are not there to work or buy.
One library,
community center,
arts space,
care provider,
or Main Street block may hold several.

**Conversation**:
enter a cafe table,
library discussion,
book group,
or meeting room with a question or shared subject.

**Hospitality**:
enter a community meal,
congregate meal site,
community kitchen,
or faith or neighborhood hall to serve,
eat,
and remain.

**Making**:
enter a library makerspace,
arts studio,
print room,
fabrication lab,
or vocational shop with material that can take form.

**Repair**:
enter a Repair Cafe,
bicycle cooperative,
tool library,
alteration shop,
or supervised repair bench with something worn or broken.

**Learning**:
enter a public library program,
community college,
adult school,
Cooperative Extension office,
apprenticeship,
or demonstration table with something to learn or teach.

**Play**:
enter a park program,
recreation center,
game table,
sports court,
dance group,
or improv class through shared rules and uncertainty.

**Performance And Celebration**:
enter a community theater,
arts center,
school auditorium,
public park event,
reading,
or music room as a participant or witness.

**Witness And Memory**:
enter a gallery,
museum,
local archive,
library display,
or neighborhood history room with something to preserve or encounter.

**Exchange**:
enter a market,
cooperative,
tool library,
library of things,
seed exchange,
book exchange,
or free pantry with something to offer or borrow.

**Care**:
enter licensed childcare,
an adult day or PACE program,
hospice care through a certified provider,
or a provider-led support space through a qualified and accountable care path.
A care doorway may require intake,
referral,
or eligibility instead of open drop-in.
Keep it findable.

**Civic Assembly**:
enter a public meeting room,
neighborhood council,
union hall,
town forum,
or library meeting space with a common matter to decide or act on.

**Contemplation**:
enter a reading room,
garden,
park,
sanctuary,
quiet craft table,
or public seat without a demand to speak or produce.

A person walking or rolling along a civic Main Street should be able to read several of these invitations.
The person can arrive with a bicycle,
question,
book,
skill,
appetite,
memory,
care need,
or wish for quiet.
A person can enter for one of these reasons before naming a need for community.

## Start With Existing Hosts

Use a host that already performs part of the work.
Look first to:

- public libraries
- parks and recreation departments
- community and senior centers
- arts centers,
  galleries,
  and community theaters
- Repair Cafes,
  bicycle cooperatives,
  tool libraries,
  and trade shops
- community kitchens,
  congregate meal sites,
  and neighborhood halls
- community colleges,
  adult schools,
  and Cooperative Extension
- licensed childcare,
  adult day,
  PACE,
  hospice,
  and other accountable care providers

Choose a host that can name who unlocks the door,
keeps hours,
maintains the room,
holds insurance,
keeps records,
and answers when harm occurs.

Add one adjacent use that the host can staff,
maintain,
insure,
make accessible,
and stop.
A library may hold meeting,
making,
display,
and quiet space.
A community center may host a meal,
repair gathering,
class,
or performance.
A repair host may add a teaching hour.
An arts space may add a shared making table.

Keep regulated care with the qualified provider.
Another host may share a building or make a referral under a clear agreement.
A library or community center may host information,
meals,
or caregiver support with the responsible provider.
It should not borrow credentials it does not have.

## Standards Follow The Doorway

A multipurpose room does not erase the standard attached to each use.

- Public access follows current disability-access law,
  building and fire rules,
  reachable transit or arrival,
  and the host's access policy.
- A public library follows its meeting-room,
  exhibit,
  privacy,
  intellectual-freedom,
  and equipment policies.
- A meal follows current local food-service rules.
  The FDA Food Code supplies a model for many United States jurisdictions,
  but the local code governs.
- A repair gathering needs tool rules,
  supervision,
  a refusal path,
  clear responsibility,
  and appropriate insurance.
- Childcare follows state or territory licensing,
  background-check,
  training,
  supervision,
  sanitation,
  and emergency requirements.
- Hospice,
  PACE,
  adult day health,
  and clinical care remain under their current provider,
  certification,
  privacy,
  health,
  and safety requirements.
- A museum or archive keeps public-trust,
  access,
  provenance,
  collections-care,
  interpretation,
  and facilities standards.
- A civic assembly names the decision,
  the public's role,
  the record,
  who has authority,
  and how a person can contest or appeal.

Use current official starting points:

- [American Library Association: meeting rooms, exhibits, and programs](https://www.ala.org/advocacy/intfreedom/spaces)
  and [makerspaces and media labs](https://www.ala.org/advocacy/intfreedom/statementspols/contentcreationQA)
- [Repair Cafe: working model](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/about/)
  and [house rules](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/house-rules/)
- [Administration for Community Living: congregate meals](https://acl.gov/senior-nutrition/congregate-meals)
  and [USDA Cooperative Extension](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/about-nifa/what-we-do/extension)
- [FDA Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code)
  and [Childcare.gov licensing](https://www.childcare.gov/consumer-education/regulated-child-care)
- [CMS hospice requirements](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-safety-standards/certification-compliance/hospices)
  and [PACE](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/medicaid-coordination/about/pace)
- [American Alliance of Museums Core Standards](https://www.aam-us.org/programs/ethics-standards-and-professional-practices/core-standards-for-museums/)
  and [National Archives preservation guidance](https://www.archives.gov/preservation)
- [National Recreation and Park Association CAPRA standards](https://www.nrpa.org/certification/accreditation/CAPRA/capra-standards/)
  and [IAP2 public-participation framework](https://www.iap2.org/resource/resmgr/core_values/WEB_8.5x11_2017_Core_Values_.pdf)
- [United States Department of Justice ADA Standards](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/)

These sources offer current starting points,
not universal permission.
Standards trail checked 2026-07-20.
Check the host's policy and the law that applies where the doorway operates.

## One Center At A Time

Several anchors can compete for the same attention.
Soup,
AI prototyping,
bicycle repair,
and a heavy civic question will not share one center with ease.

Choose one primary anchor and keep any other anchor light.

- coffee with a shared image
- soup with one open question
- a repair object with room for conversation
- quiet craft with food afterward

Let the center change across time.
The same table can hold an image review one morning,
a meal another evening,
and a repair session on the weekend.
The room stays familiar while the verb changes.

## Entry, Role, And Return

A person should be able to enter through observation,
quiet company,
one bounded task,
or a seat at the edge.
Do not make disclosure the admission price.

Offer roles small enough to accept and real enough to matter:
hold the part,
serve the soup,
mark the page,
watch the time,
clean the tool,
or leave a note for the next group.

Do not turn usefulness into a worth test.
A person may need to eat,
listen,
rest,
or remain quiet.
Standing can grow without forced productivity.

When people meet again,
recognition can accumulate.
Keep a repeatable time,
a visible unfinished task,
a cared-for object,
a shared record,
or another modest reason to return.
People find consequence when they can see what continued,
what changed,
and where their part belongs.

## AI And Shared Screens

Place an AI screen on the table as shared equipment.
Do not treat it as another personality in the group.
Do not rank a person's standing against the system's output.

Keep one shared display visible to the people affected.
Let them point,
compare,
mark paper,
question the output,
and decide when to turn the screen off.

AI may help produce options,
translations,
references,
or rough images.
It should not take the roles that allow people to notice one another,
hold uncertainty,
make the decision,
or carry the work forward.

## Gathering Anchors As Five Returns

**Body** asks what pace,
noise,
food,
posture,
movement,
sensory load,
access,
and rest the gathering requires.

**Record** asks what question,
note,
object state,
instruction,
decision,
or unfinished task should remain for the next meeting.

**Consequence** asks who prepares,
serves,
cleans,
maintains,
pays,
waits,
gets exposed,
or repairs harm.

**Commons** asks who can enter,
observe,
join,
question,
refuse,
correct,
leave,
and return.

**Capacity** asks whether people leave more able to meet,
make,
teach,
care,
repair,
decide,
or rest together.

## Drill: One Host, One Doorway, One Return

Choose one real gathering.
Set a ten-minute timer.

1. Name the existing host.
2. Name its current civic doorway.
3. Name one adjacent doorway that this kind of host already supports elsewhere.
4. Name the policy,
   code,
   license,
   qualified role,
   or professional standard that governs the added use.
5. Name the primary anchor: attention, hospitality, stewardship, or creation.
6. Name one way to join without speaking or disclosing private pain.
7. Name who prepares,
   maintains,
   cleans,
   records,
   and pays.
8. Name one reason to return at a known time.
9. Stop if the added use requires authority or credentials the host does not have.

End with one sentence:
which existing door can carry this use without tearing its standard?

## Test Questions

- Can someone tell what the gathering is for without being recruited into a performance?
- Can a quiet or uncertain person take part?
- Which existing host carries the doorway?
- Which current standard governs the use?
- Can the host add this use without borrowing authority or credentials?
- Does one primary anchor organize the room?
- Can people question or turn off a shared screen?
- Are preparation and maintenance visible and shared?
- Can roles change without creating a permanent expert or permanent recipient?
- Does anything continue between meetings?
- Can a person leave by choice and return without penalty?

## Stop Conditions

Stop or revise if the anchor becomes a pretext for forced disclosure,
unpaid exhaustion,
expert dominance,
content harvesting,
sales pressure,
or surveillance.

Stop if food,
tools,
screens,
noise,
cost,
transit,
stairs,
language,
or scheduling exclude people while the gathering claims openness.

Stop if the task becomes a worth test or the room treats quiet presence as failure.

Stop if a new use bypasses the host's current policy,
license,
qualified staff,
insurance,
access duty,
or safety standard.

Stop if people focus on AI output at the expense of one another,
or if the system hides the source or decision path.
Stop if it replaces the human roles that give the gathering consequence.

Stop if the gathering needs clinical care,
crisis support,
legal advice,
safety intervention,
or another accountable service.
Move to a qualified person or responsible process.

People can share something real before anyone asks them to explain themselves.
