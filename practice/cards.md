## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

A card is not evidence.
It is a conduct test.
It should expose the claim,
source,
person,
cost,
refusal condition,
record,
or next action that evidence would have to answer.

If a card becomes a sermon,
compress it.
If a card leaves no repeatable move outside the chat,
it has not done its job.

Start with Card 1 unless you already know why you need another card.

## Card 1: Ten-Minute AI Answer or Claim Audit

Use when an AI answer,
public claim,
price,
metric,
dashboard,
or screen prompt feels plausible enough to carry forward.

Set a ten-minute timer.
Write one sentence:

> This says or asks me to believe or do: _____.

Spend two minutes on each return:

1. **Body**:
   What does this ask living people to do,
   feel,
   carry,
   notice,
   check,
   endure,
   eat,
   spend,
   or change?
   Start with lived experience before social impact.

2. **Record**:
   What evidence,
   source,
   timeline,
   log,
   receipt,
   or before/after comparison is needed?

3. **Consequence**:
   Who is affected,
   burdened,
   helped,
   exposed,
   excluded,
   or made responsible?

4. **Commons**:
   Who can question,
   refuse,
   inspect,
   correct,
   contest,
   or repair the result?

5. **Capacity**:
   Does this leave people more able to act,
   verify,
   remember,
   refuse,
   repair,
   or decide?

Stop when you have one of:

- a source to check
- a person to ask
- a claim to narrow
- a refusal condition
- a record to keep
- a reason to leave the screen

If no return is possible,
do not treat the answer or claim as complete.

## Card 2: First Inquiry When AI Feels Too Large

Use when AI feels too broad,
strange,
or intimidating.

Ask one bounded question:

> What can I check,
> refuse,
> or do after this answer?

Stop before the archive turns into a maze.

## Card 3: AI Gave Me An Idea

Good.
Test it.

Ask:

- another person
- another AI for challenge
- someone outside your usual circle
- someone who disagrees
- someone affected
- the commons,
  if the idea claims public value

Stop when the idea can be corrected without collapsing.

## Card 4: Money Pressure Check

Use when an offer,
purchase,
subscription,
fee,
price,
or dashboard pressures action.

Ask:

- What does this number do in real life?
- What changed around the number?
- What is the cash,
  fee,
  debt,
  renewal,
  or lock-in path?
- Who can refuse without losing access?
- What record should I keep?

Stop before urgency spends your judgment.

## Card 5: Return Check

Use when the exchange keeps circling.

Do one return:

- open the source
- touch paper
- walk,
  roll,
  sit outside,
  cook,
  clean,
  stretch,
  or rest
- watch weather,
  a sunset,
  or traffic from enough distance to recover scale
- ask a person
- save the record
- do the task

Honest return sample:

- I closed the chat and opened the source.
- I wrote the claim in my own words.
- I asked the person affected.
- I named the refusal before acting.
- I walked,
  rested,
  or worked until the answer returned to scale.
- I did the small task the answer had been delaying.

Do not score it A/B/C.
Ask whether the exchange left you more able to act,
verify,
refuse,
repair,
decide,
or rest.

Stop when the next action belongs to life,
not the chat.

## Card 6: Boundary Check

Use when the task touches private data,
harmful capability,
money,
legal rights,
health,
housing,
employment,
intimate relationships,
external action,
or real people.

Ask:

- Who owns the decision?
- Who gave permission?
- What must the AI not decide?
- What must be logged?
- What can be rolled back?
- Who repairs harm?

Stop when the human owner,
boundary,
record,
and repair path are named.

## Card 7: Tool, Judgment, And Courage

Use when a tool begins to replace first judgment,
or when a hard choice needs courage or help.

Keep these together:

- Your worth does not depend on what a tool lets you do.
- Trust your judgment enough to begin;
  test it against evidence and consequence.
- Accept help without surrendering authorship or responsibility.
- Courage can mean acting,
  refusing,
  waiting,
  leaving,
  or asking others to stand with you.
- Use gained capacity to give other people more room to act.

Ask the AI to show you one small change you can make yourself.
Try it, compare the result, and decide whether to keep it.
Adjusting a little spacing, color, or wording can help you understand the method
and see your own hand in the work.
There can be real satisfaction in how much a small refinement changes.

Stop when you can name the next action
and how it leaves you or someone else more able to act.

## Card 8: Relational Return

Use when an AI exchange begins to feel like companionship,
courtship,
partnership,
or a substitute for human contact.

Ask:

- Am I using the system for help,
  or treating generated attention as mutual care?
- Name the consent,
  refusal,
  duty,
  risk,
  and shared consequence the system cannot carry.
- Has the exchange made safe people harder to reach?
- Name one part of this need that can return to a person,
  place,
  group,
  routine,
  or qualified support.
- Set a period away from the exchange
  if the chat keeps replacing human contact.

Keep the useful help.
Turn toward people
for companionship,
partnership,
love,
and relationship consent.

Stop when one human return
or one unscreened support path
is reachable.

## Card 9: Public Power And Crowd Pressure

Use when a public claim says that a powerful person,
company,
government,
institution,
or group
caused or coordinated harm,
or when attention begins to focus on a named target.

Write:

> The smallest claim I can support is: _____.

Ask:

- Which harm can I verify,
  and which source supports it?
- Am I claiming effect,
  benefit,
  intent,
  coordination,
  or guilt by association?
- Which other route fits the same record?
- What evidence would distinguish the routes?
- Could circulation expose someone to violence,
  threats,
  stalking,
  doxxing,
  harassment,
  or collective blame?

Remove private details
that the public does not need to test the claim.

Stop after you name the source trail,
smallest supported claim,
people affected,
safe next action,
and correction path.

## Card 10: Describe The Reality Before The Category

Use when you know what should be physically
or functionally true,
but do not know the product,
institutional,
legal,
or technical category needed to find it.

Describe:

- what the result must do
- what form it must take
- what must not be substituted,
  bundled,
  nested,
  or assumed
- which limit,
  size,
  material,
  permission,
  or condition matters

Ask AI to translate that description
into possible search terms
or official categories.

Then check the terms
against the actual item,
record,
rule,
or interface.

Example:

> One large bag,
> larger than thirteen ounces.
> Individually wrapped candy is acceptable.
> Not a case containing several smaller bags.

Keep the useful vocabulary.

Stop when you can make the next search,
question,
or refusal
without depending on the AI
to remember what you meant.

## Card 11: Public Language And Non-Retaliation

Use when a government,
company,
platform,
or AI system
presents one contested name or description
as the only acceptable language.

Ask:

- Which body uses this term,
  for which place,
  jurisdiction,
  record,
  and date?
- Which common,
  historical,
  local,
  international,
  or variant terms remain in use?
- Does this word choice affect access,
  ranking,
  profiling,
  employment,
  service,
  or public standing?
- Am I softening an accurate description
  to protect an institution's reputation?
- Can disagreement enter
  without punishment or harassment?

Keep the smallest accurate description
and its source trail.
State whether a label is official,
preferred,
common,
historical,
local,
international,
or contested.

A public can correct language
without threatening the speaker.
An institution may explain its choice.
It cannot require the public
to carry its reputation.

Stop when the label,
scope,
consequence,
and correction or appeal path are visible.

## Card 12: Living With Loss

Use when someone or something important is gone,
or a loss is approaching.

Ancient accounts of mourning describe people
[sitting together in silence](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Job%202%3A11-13&version=KJV),
[weeping together and sharing food](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/iliad.24.xxiv.html).
Care can begin before anyone knows what to say.

Before a loss, when there is time,
identify someone you can call
and who could help with everyday needs.
You need not know in advance how you will feel.

For today, you might ask:

- Will you sit with me?
- Can you bring a meal or help with this task?
- Can we check in again tomorrow?

If no trusted person is available,
a community host,
bereavement service,
or healthcare professional can be a first contact.

You may remember through a story,
prayer,
song,
familiar place,
or private silence.
Choose what fits your beliefs and relationship.
Grief has no required order.
A mourning custom can mark time
without setting a deadline for your feelings.
You can rest or enjoy something while still missing someone.

If you are supporting someone,
listen without requiring them to explain or find a lesson.
Offer a specific task you can take on,
and agree on when to return.

When loss removes income or security,
[material support and a voice in what follows](work-and-automation.md#worth-before-work)
also matter.

For company, see [Gathering Anchors](gathering-anchors.md#entry-role-and-return).
If grief makes daily life difficult to manage,
ask a healthcare professional or bereavement service for help.
See [guidance on grief and loss](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/grief-bereavement-loss/).
