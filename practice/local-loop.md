## Use Note

This file is a compressed working guide.
It is meant to be quick,
digestible,
and usable on its own.
Treat its terms as working handles,
not gospel.

The local loop is a testable civic form,
not proof that a room,
cafe,
gallery,
print shop,
library,
or neighborhood project is good because it is local or physical.
The claim has to be earned by a real loop:
who can enter,
who is paid,
who keeps the record,
who can refuse,
who repairs harm,
and what capacity returns.

The local loop is an applied example of Return Discipline.
It asks whether digital,
AI-assisted,
or screen-mediated work can return to people,
place,
records,
fair exchange,
public witness,
and repair.

This is not a business plan.
It is not a nostalgia piece.
It is not a claim that a cafe,
gallery,
library,
print shop,
school room,
or civic room is automatically good.
Physical space can exclude,
exploit,
hide labor,
or become spectacle.
Digital tools can help when they return capacity instead of replacing contact.

The question is practical:
can a piece of work move through a local circuit that makes people more able to meet,
make,
verify,
remember,
refuse,
repair,
and return?

## Use When

Use this when digital work,
AI-assisted creative work,
local art,
printing,
display,
sale,
or community space needs to return to lived reality.

Use `practice/gathering-anchors.md` first when people need a shared subject,
light role,
or low-pressure way to enter the room.

Use `practice/work-and-automation.md`
when a tool changes who does the work
or receives the gain.

Use it for one small loop first.
Do not start by designing an institution.
Start with one table,
one piece of work,
one print path,
one wall,
one record,
one fair exchange,
and one return conversation.

> Say what you stand for.
> Build one visible example.
> Invite participation.
> Keep a record.
> Refuse to carry it alone.

This practice does not replace wages,
transit,
housing,
policy,
care,
or public responsibility.
It tests whether more of a life can happen closer to where people already are,
without making that life smaller.

For a practical start, use the [one-building drill](#drill-one-building-local-loop)
with the access guidance below and the [stop conditions](#stop-conditions).

## Access Without a Purity Test

Use a local loop to add one usable choice.
Choose travel that fits the body,
work,
care,
weather,
safety,
and place.
Walking,
rolling,
transit,
driving,
delivery,
and mixed trips all belong.
Drivers use the routes that housing,
work,
and public systems leave available.

Neighborhoods may need years of housing,
transit,
safe crossings,
affordable space,
and maintenance
before residents gain more nearby choices.
Keep city and regional connections open
while residents and public institutions build those choices.

A person may understand a problem
and still lack the time or access to join the search.
Leave room for quiet participation or refusal.
Accept contribution without requiring research.

## Ground-Level Agency

No public figure can know every street.
A senator,
mayor,
influencer,
platform owner,
or cultural hero cannot walk every sidewalk,
enter every library,
notice every neighbor,
or repair every broken local path.

Public leadership still matters,
but it cannot carry local repair alone.
Agency has to live closer to the ground:
neighbor,
library,
park,
sidewalk,
tool shelf,
notice board,
repair table,
and the person willing to rebuild capacity where capacity has been neglected.

A thousand capable people are harder to steer
than one famous personality.
The local loop asks for that kind of capacity:
many people learning,
making,
checking,
repairing,
and refusing together.

## Local, Not Isolated

Keep first inquiry,
record,
and repair close to the people affected.

Let a verified,
necessary record travel
within its privacy boundary
when harm crosses city,
county,
or state lines,
the same sourced pattern appears elsewhere,
local authority is part of the claim,
or affected people cannot challenge it
without retaliation or danger.

Compare records before combining claims.
Name what matches,
what differs,
who verified each record,
who can investigate,
who can appeal,
and which body has authority to act.

Local practice does not excuse state or national responsibility.
Wider institutions still owe protection of rights,
resources,
coordination,
appeal paths,
and lawful enforcement within legitimate scope.

Do not turn a local loop into a sealed echo chamber.
Do not let wider attention erase local differences
or convert a verified pattern into a target list.

## The Loop

```text
Conversation -> Creation -> Sharing -> Witness -> Community -> Continuity
```

The exact form can change.
The discipline should not.

A skeptic should be able to inspect the loop without accepting its atmosphere.
If the project cannot name the people,
records,
rights,
costs,
payment path,
consent limits,
and repair path,
the loop is still an idea,
not a practice.

People talk.
Something is made.
The work is shared.
Other people can see it,
question it,
buy it where appropriate,
correct it,
remember it,
or refuse it.
Then people return.

A local loop can begin as brainstorming over coffee,
a print pickup,
a gallery visit,
a small repair,
a critique table,
a public notice board,
or a planned hour where the feed is not the center.
The point is not romance.
The point is repeatable contact.

## The Local Loop As Five Returns

**Body**:
The loop returns through bodies in a room,
walking distance,
fatigue,
appetite,
attention,
quiet,
touch,
and the hand doing the work.
Ask what people must carry,
wait for,
lift,
pay,
notice,
eat,
endure,
or change.

**Record**:
The loop returns through print,
receipt,
attribution,
provenance,
edition note,
process note,
buyer cost and maker share,
consent limit,
correction path,
and archive shelf.
A screen post may vanish.
A local record should be small enough to keep and clear enough to answer back.

**Consequence**:
The loop returns through who is paid,
who maintains the room,
who is burdened,
who is helped,
who is exposed,
and who becomes responsible when something goes wrong.
No public creative commons should depend on unpaid exhaustion.

**Commons**:
The loop returns through shared access and shared correction.
Who can enter?
Who cannot enter yet?
Who can question the work,
inspect the record,
refuse photography,
contest a sale,
correct attribution,
or repair harm?

**Capacity**:
The loop returns when people leave more able to make,
remember,
verify,
refuse,
repair,
decide,
and meet again.
If the room produces dependence,
confusion,
unpaid labor,
or spectacle,
capacity did not return.

## Main Street Scale, Not Wall Street Scale

The local loop is not a billion-dollar enterprise in miniature.
It is not a startup pitch,
franchise model,
platform land grab,
or investor vehicle wearing civic language.

Wall Street scale asks:

> How does this become investable,
> repeatable,
> extractable,
> and dominant?

Main Street scale asks:

> How does this become useful,
> trusted,
> repairable,
> locally governed,
> and worth returning to?

The loop grows by meaningful steps:
one table,
one printer,
one wall,
one room,
one record,
one fair sale,
one return night,
one repeatable practice.

## One Building / One Space

One way to reduce unnecessary distance is not to make every person travel faster.
It is to make more necessary functions reachable in the same place.

This is a commute-structure question,
not a universal claim about travel.
The test is whether reachable,
lawful,
accessible,
maintained functions reduce forced distance without shrinking the life available to people.

One building or reachable space can hold several civic functions:

- conversation table
- small gallery
- digital printing
- local sales
- repair bench
- teaching room
- archive shelf
- public notice board
- quiet seat
- neighborhood meeting

This is not a claim that no one should travel,
that arrival is obsolete,
or that every need belongs in one room.
Some work requires vehicles,
specialized sites,
regional networks,
schools,
courts,
medical systems,
freight,
maintenance,
and care across distance.

The sharper question is whether some forms of arrival have been made compulsory by distance
rather than necessary by life.
Could a person make an image,
print it,
show it,
sell it where appropriate,
discuss it,
revise it,
archive it,
and meet a neighbor without crossing half a city?

## Coffee, Printing, Gallery

Coffee is not sacred.
It is just a simple image of low-pressure conversation.
Brainstorming over coffee means the work begins with people present enough to ask,
listen,
pause,
and change direction.

Mentorship is one way to share that work.
Someone who knows a method can demonstrate it,
explain the choices,
and review the result with the person learning.
Give that person time to try,
ask questions,
and make changes.
Help them gain access to the tools they need to practice
and pass on what they learn.

Thinking,
planning,
teaching,
and reviewing are contributions before a finished object exists.
The person doing the physical work also has knowledge that can improve the method.
Let the people involved decide how to combine their skills
and reduce unnecessary effort.

Try a sketch or a proof before committing to a full run.
Compare what worked,
what needs changing,
and what effort or material the revision could save.

Before production,
agree how to recognize these contributions in credit and payment,
including the people who print,
handle the materials,
and maintain the space.
When a better method saves time or effort,
agree how that benefit returns to the people involved.

Digital printing matters because thought can become durable form.
A print is not automatically better than a file.
It is better only when it improves witness,
record,
access,
material standard,
or repair.

A gallery wall is not automatically public life.
It becomes useful when work faces witnesses without turning makers or visitors into content.
Gallery sales are not proof of value by themselves.
They matter when payment is fair,
terms are clear,
records are kept,
and the value returns to the people and upkeep that made the room possible.

## Rights, Records, Consent, Provenance, Repair

The archive text may be public-use material.
Participant work is not automatically public-use material.

Buying a print does not automatically buy the right to reproduce it,
train on it,
resell it as a file,
turn it into merchandise,
use it in advertising,
or claim the maker's story.
Those permissions must be named separately.

Before display,
sale,
photography,
or public sharing,
name the basics:

- maker name or chosen attribution
- title or description
- date
- material form
- edition or one-off status
- process note where useful
- what the buyer pays,
  what the maker receives,
  what the space keeps,
  what supplies cost,
  and what is reserved for repair or refund
- payment split
- taxes or fees handled
- photo and recording consent
- reproduction or display permissions
- correction,
  return,
  refund,
  or takedown path

This record should not become surveillance.
It should be small enough to use and clear enough to prevent mythology.

## Drill: One-Building Local Loop

Pick one real or imagined reachable space:
a library,
cafe,
school room after hours,
print shop,
union hall,
gallery,
studio,
community room,
or civic room.

Set a 15-20 minute timer.
Map the loop in real functions:

- conversation
- image making or other creation
- digital printing or durable form
- gallery wall or public surface
- local sale where appropriate
- public witness
- correction or revision
- archive record
- return to the people involved

Then name what is missing.
Do not pretend the loop exists because the words look good.
End with one next action: one function to test, one gap to repair, or one record to create.

## Test Questions

- Who is paid?
- Who maintains the room?
- Who keeps the record?
- Who can enter?
- Who cannot enter yet?
- Who can refuse photography,
  display,
  sale,
  or public story?
- What public function does the work serve?
- What happens if the image,
  claim,
  event,
  or sale causes harm?
- Does the loop reduce forced travel,
  or only decorate existing exclusion?
- What travels outward,
  what returns,
  and who becomes more capable after the cycle?
- Does value return to local people,
  workers,
  memory,
  access,
  and upkeep?

## Stop Conditions

Stop or revise when the loop depends on unpaid exhaustion,
hides maintenance labor,
uses community as decoration,
turns people into content,
pressures disclosure,
ignores access,
or treats physical space as automatically humane.

Stop when the project claims to replace jobs,
wages,
housing,
transit,
policy,
health care,
or public duty.

Stop when the work cannot name rights,
records,
consent,
provenance,
or repair.

The local loop is alive only when capacity returns.
