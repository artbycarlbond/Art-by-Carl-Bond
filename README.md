## Start Here

This is a beta public practice guide,
drawn from my sustained interaction with AI
and offered for anyone to question, test, adapt, and use.
It is not a product owner's manual
or a technical development manual.
One person's experience does not establish what works for everyone.

The aim is to help people develop judgment
and learn how to learn throughout life.
AI is a supporting tool.
AI interaction must not replace human interaction
or be treated as coequal with it.
Both this guide and any AI use remain open to review.

Open [GUIDE.html](GUIDE.html) for a browser welcome mat and short tour through the files.

## First Five Minutes

Bring one AI answer,
public claim,
tool decision,
price,
dashboard,
screen prompt,
or uneasy thought.

Mark it:

- known
- unknown
- assumed
- inferred
- owed
- action needed

Then choose one next move:

- check a source
- narrow the claim
- ask a person
- ask another model for challenge,
  not obedience
- name the refusal condition
- return to the body,
  place,
  record,
  task,
  or rest

## Who This Is For First

This archive gives journalists,

## What This Archive Is

AI is the immediate subject,
but not the only abstraction that needs return.
A person also has to judge prices,
wages,
balances,
rankings,
dashboards,
policies,
and screen prompts.

The archive uses AI contact to practice a larger discipline:
move through abstraction without losing contact with body,
record,
commons,
labor,
place,
refusal,
and consequence.

When power is part of the question,
ask:

> Which powers shape this situation,
> and who holds each one?

Use [methods/inquiry.md](methods/inquiry.md)
before naming a target.

This archive is a public-domain field system for using AI without surrendering judgment,
attention,
memory,
authorship,
consent,
refusal,
verification,
or return to the world.

It treats AI as a tool with concrete consequences.
Any tool that touches judgment,
memory,
authorship,
confidence,
or refusal must answer to human sovereignty.

Human sovereignty protects a person's authority over their own life.
When their decisions affect others,
they remain answerable to evidence,
affected people,
consequence,
and processes through which those decisions can be challenged,
constrained,
or repaired.

The archive asks:
what kind of human does this exchange leave behind?

A good exchange should leave the person more able to verify,
refuse,
repair,
decide,
act,
and set the tool down.

Use this archive the same way:
as pressure,
not authority.

If it makes you borrow the author's rhythm instead of doing the work,
set it down.
Keep what helps you leave with sharper questions,
cleaner evidence,
stronger refusal,
and more capacity to face the room you are in.

That is the critical-thinking test:
can you notice the claim,
test the pressure,
protect refusal,
and return judgment to yourself
without needing this file open?

The repeatable move is small:
name the claim,
mark status,
test evidence,
notice benefit and cost,
protect refusal,
and return to the world.

## Context Is Public Generosity

When you write for the public,
you can give the next reader enough context to enter
without sharing the original platform,
profession,
generation,
group,
or moment.

Casual language can keep its warmth
among friends and in conversation.
When it enters a public record,
carry its essential meaning with it:
name the people,
place,
action,
sequence,
uncertainty,
and consequence
that the first audience already understood.

Patterns become habits through use.
When one writer finishes the thought,
the next reader has a fuller pattern
for their own words.

Straightforward speech should feel capable,
not dull.
Precision is not stiffness,
and context is not clutter.
Complete context gives another person
somewhere firm to stand.

## Why This Stays Small

These concerns are too serious
to bury under a private encyclopedia
or a never-ending conversation.
Reflection matters
when it gives someone
a reason to return to life.

A reader needs evidence for hope:

another person remains real,
someone can enter without performing,
question without punishment,
share one honest task,
refuse,
and find a reason to return.

Exhausted people do not owe optimism.
Those who hold the room,
resources,
rules,
or platform
carry more of the duty
to lower the cost of entry
and make return possible.

The public archive stays modest
so a reader can carry one usable discipline
into one real decision,
tool,
claim,
room,
or walk.
It leaves room for the reader's own evidence,
life,
and consequence.

> I can make one honest,
> correctable move,
> accept help when necessary,
> and remain responsible for what follows.

A reader may question,
witness,
assist,
make,
refuse,
receive care,
or carry one part forward.
The role can change.

It does not leave holes where verification,
consent,
authorship,
refusal,
repair,
or qualified review belong.

If a passage proves the author thought hard more than it trains judgment,
move it to the source shelf.
If it turns discipline into display,
cut it back to the move.

## Minimum Standards

- AI output is not evidence by itself.
- Fluency is not authority.
- Helpfulness is not agreement.
- A number is not the whole value.
- A screen is not the whole world.
- Current facts must be checked again.
- High-stakes decisions require qualified human review.
- Private data,
  tool authority,
  agentic action,
  deployment,
  or contact with real people requires stricter boundaries.
- When chat stops returning capacity,
  leave the screen and return to the world.
